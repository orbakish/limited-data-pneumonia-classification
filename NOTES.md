# Project Notes

A shared running log of open questions, decisions, and ideas — so nothing gets lost between
work sessions or between us.

**How to use this file:**
- **Open questions** — anything that needs a decision or an answer from the other person.
  Note who it's for and its status.
- **Decisions made** — once resolved, move it here *with the reasoning*, so we don't
  re-litigate it later or forget why we chose it.
- **Parking lot** — ideas worth remembering but deliberately not doing now.

Question numbers are stable IDs: once a question is answered it moves to Decisions, and its
number is never reused. Gaps in the numbering are normal.

Add to it freely as things come up rather than waiting until the end of a session.

| **Grad-CAM stability is measured two ways.** *In-family:* a small rotation, with the perturbed image's heatmap rotated back into alignment and the out-of-frame corners masked before comparing. *Out-of-family:* small Gaussian noise. Both are reported, along with the gap between them. | Augmentation trains a model to ignore specific changes, so a perturbation from that same family makes augmented models look more stable by construction - the metric would partly measure overlap between training augmentation and test perturbation, and would tilt the result against the proposal's own hypothesis that strong augmentation may reduce stability. Noise is outside every regime and moves no pixels, so it is the fair comparison. The in-family measurement is kept because the gap shows how much apparent stability is trained-in invariance. Rotated heatmaps need re-alignment, or even a perfectly consistent model would score low because the lungs moved. **How it is measured (implemented 2026-09-26, `src/stability.py`; choices by Yotam, reviewed by Fable):** on a **fixed test subset** - every NORMAL test image and 234 of the 390 PNEUMONIA ones, drawn once by seed from the filenames alone, before any model was scored on them, and committed as `data/splits/stability_subset.txt`. Every map explains the image's **true class**, so all 27 configs answer the same question on the same image (with the predicted class, models that disagree on an image would be explaining different things). **In-family:** rotation by +5 and -5 degrees (mild's bound, so in-family for both augmented regimes; the same bilinear kernel, centre and black fill as training), the perturbed map rotated back, and only pixels that stay inside the frame through both rotations compared. **Out-of-family:** Gaussian noise added in 0-1 pixel space and clipped, one grayscale draw copied to the three channels (independent channel noise would add a colour shift no training image had), two draws per image, seeded so every config sees the same noise; its size is fixed by the calibration in #7. **Similarity: Pearson correlation** of the upsampled maps (cosine stored beside it). Grad-CAM maps are non-negative, so cosine scores two unrelated maps around 0.5-0.8 and would compress every difference; Pearson's chance level is about 0 (for unrelated maps - two same-class images' maps correlate far more, 0.54 in E01; see "How the stability results are read"). An image's score is the mean over its two passes per family; maps that are empty or constant give no score, and are counted. The headline in-family, out-of-family and **gap** are means over the images scored in both families, each with a 95% patient-group bootstrap interval (the gap's from paired resamples). Beside them, in a `stability` block: the same means over correctly classified images only (a true-class map on a misclassified image explains a class the model rejected, so stability and accuracy are partly entangled), the prediction-flip rate per family, the mean pixel change per family (a model-free size), a chance baseline (each clean map against another same-class image's), and the cosine means. Per-image results and the raw 7x7 maps are saved beside each checkpoint, so any of this can be recomputed after the sweep without retraining. **Limitations:** a 7x7 corner cell is only partly masked; clipping lifts near-black pixels by about 0.4 sigma; the rotation is applied to the float image rather than the 8-bit one training rotates (a quantisation-level difference). | 2026-09-13, implemented 2026-09-26 |

| **How Grad-CAM is computed** (`src/gradcam.py`, Selvaraju et al. equations 1-2).
