# Project Notes

A shared running log of open questions, decisions, and ideas — so nothing gets lost between
work sessions or between us.

**How to use this file:**
- **Open questions** — anything that needs a decision or an answer from the other person.
  Note who it's for and its status.
- **Decisions made** — once resolved, move it here *with the reasoning*, so we don't
  re-litigate it later or forget why we chose it.
- **Parking lot** — ideas worth remembering but deliberately not doing now.

Question numbers are stable IDs: once a question is answered it moves to Decisions, and its
number is never reused. Gaps in the numbering are normal.

Add to it freely as things come up rather than waiting until the end of a session.

| **Transfer learning by fine-tuning (question #6).** Default: **full fine-tuning** - every layer trains. A tuning run tests **partial** fine-tuning (earlier layers frozen), with the boundary defined per architecture as **every block at the final 7x7 resolution, plus the classifier**: `layer4` + `fc` in ResNet18, `features.9`-`features.12` + `classifier` in MobileNetV3-Small, `features.6`-`features.8` + `classifier` in EfficientNet-B0 (recorded in `ARCHITECTURES` in `src/models.py`). | **Training recipe (question #18)**, identical for all three architectures and all 27 runs: AdamW with weight decay 1e-4; learning rate split into 1e-3 for the new head and 1e-4 for the pretrained backbone (the head is only the newly initialised output layer, so MobileNetV3-Small's pretrained 576->1024 classifier layer trains at the backbone rate); constant schedule; batch size 32; at most 30 epochs with early stopping after 5 epochs without improvement; early stopping and the saved checkpoint follow **validation balanced accuracy** (revised 2026-09-16; originally validation ROC-AUC); validation loss logged every epoch. | The new head starts random while the backbone is already good, so a split rate lets the head learn quickly without its early, noisy gradients overwriting pretrained features. A constant rate gives every run identical optimisation behaviour, whereas plateau schedules react to each run's validation noise. **Original reasoning for ROC-AUC (2026-09-15):** ROC-AUC is threshold-free; validation loss can rise from overconfidence while separation still improves. **Why selection moved to balanced accuracy (2026-09-16, from T01 on Colab):** validation ROC-AUC sat at 0.995-0.996 from epoch 5 onwards, a spread smaller than its own 95% interval (+-0.0025), while balanced accuracy was still climbing from 0.92 to 0.95. Selecting on ROC-AUC kept the epoch-7 checkpoint (balanced accuracy 0.9175) over epoch 10's (0.9493), winning by 0.0006 on a saturated metric. ROC-AUC is still reported everywhere. **Caveats:** (1) the dataset is known to be easy, so ROC-AUC may saturate near 0.98-0.99 and leave little headroom - the main signal may then be Grad-CAM stability. **Confirmed on T01**, at an even higher 0.995-0.996, which is what moved selection to balanced accuracy; (2) a 20% epoch is about 28 updates against about 139 at 100%, so patience in epochs is more generous at larger sizes - the tuning phase checks that 20% runs stop before the cap and larger runs do not stop prematurely; (3) "identical recipe" does not mean identical architectures: MobileNetV3-Small and EfficientNet-B0 have 0.2 dropout in their heads (ResNet18 has none) and EfficientNet-B0 uses stochastic depth; (4) GPU determinism is settled in the training-implementation decision below. | 2026-09-15, revised 2026-09-16 |

All on `train_20`, no augmentation, seed 42, judged on the validation set only - the test set stays untouched. Both comparisons - fine-tuning depth (T04-T06) and class weighting (T07-T09) - are judged by **balanced accuracy**, with ROC-AUC secondary (revised 2026-09-25; fine-tuning depth was first to be judged by ROC-AUC with balanced accuracy as tiebreak).
